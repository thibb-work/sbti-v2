# LinkedIn post — SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2

**Audience:** energy professionals and sustainability teams working in-house at brands
**Style:** plain-language update

---

The SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2 just landed, and it's a genuine shift in how corporate climate targets work — not a tidy-up. The headline change: Scopes 1, 2 and 3 each get their own standalone target, so a flat supply chain can no longer hide behind a clean grid. Scope 1 now runs on 100% coverage and three clear routes — absolute cuts, sector intensity pathways, or a new asset-transition plan to retire and replace high-carbon equipment. Scope 2 broadens what counts as "clean" to low-carbon electricity (renewables, nuclear, CCS) but tightens the rules underneath it: matching moves from annual to hourly — climbing from 50% to 90% by 2035 — inside real deliverability regions, with purchased instruments tracked separately from your physical inventory. And Scope 3 stops being a "cover two-thirds and tick the box" exercise and becomes a decision system: define the boundary, pick the right lever for each category, and deliver through a ranked hierarchy that always starts with cutting emissions at the source.

Here's why I think it's the right call. v2 is built around one idea — decarbonize the real economy, not the spreadsheet. Reductions you make inside your own value chain earn company-level claims. Actions you take through shared systems, supply sheds or sector-wide efforts earn a separate "system contribution" claim — reported alongside, never blended in. That single distinction does a lot of work: it rewards the brands actually changing how energy is generated and how goods are made, and it makes it far harder to wave a green flag with a stack of cheap certificates. For energy and sustainability teams, the takeaway is simple — the standard now showcases who does more, and quietly asks everyone else to show their work. Worth getting ahead of: it's published now, effective February 2027, and v1 closes to new targets at the end of 2027.
